Research

Intent-Aware Robot Adaptation in Collaborative Stacking

Independent research · ongoing

How can a robot adapt when a human partner steps into a shared task, without disrupting their flow, and still complete the task? In collaborative stacking, a vision-language-action policy (SmolVLA) performs the stacking while a planner and monitor track what the human has changed and re-plan the remaining steps. Built in Isaac Sim on a Franka arm and trained on scripted expert demonstrations. Evaluation with human interjections is in progress, for submission to HRI 2027 Late-Breaking Reports.

Memory-Augmented MPC for Human-Following Robot in Cluttered Environments

International Conference on Ubiquitous Robots (UR), 2025 — Poster
paper

MAMPC is a human-following controller with temporal memory: a sliding window of recent optimal control inputs and their collision costs lets the MPC refine high-risk parts of its horizon and follow more smoothly. An elliptical personal-space model (personal, robot-buffer and social zones) sets how close the robot may come. In simulation (2D and Gazebo, Freight100 robot), across 150 trials in six cluttered environments, MAMPC succeeded in 98% of trials versus 20% for standard MPC, keeping a 2.5 ± 0.3 m following distance.

Eye Gaze as a Predictor of Human Navigation Intent

Fluent Robotics Lab, University of Michigan, 2024 Advised by Prof. Christoforos Mavrogiannis

Explored whether gaze features (fixation duration, saccade direction, goal bias) help predict where a person will walk, using the MoGaze and THÖR-MAGNI motion-capture and eye-tracking datasets. The main challenges were noisy gaze signals and the scarcity of open datasets that capture gaze during navigation, which limited what could be concluded and pointed to the need for richer real-world gaze data.
